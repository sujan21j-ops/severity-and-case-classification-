## Pharmacovigilance Classification

### Serious Adverse Drug Reactions

An ADR is considered serious if it results in:

- Death
- Life-threatening condition
- Hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization
- Persistent or significant disability
- Congenital anomaly or birth defect
- Other medically important conditions

### Non-Serious Adverse Drug Reactions

ADRs that do not meet the regulatory criteria for seriousness are classified as non-serious. They may require treatment or monitoring but generally do not result in major clinical consequences.

### Expected Adverse Drug Reactions

An ADR is considered expected when its nature and severity are consistent with the applicable reference safety information, such as the approved product information.

### Unexpected Adverse Drug Reactions

An ADR is considered unexpected when its nature, severity, or outcome is inconsistent with the applicable reference safety information.

## Conclusion

The evaluation of the five ADR cases demonstrates the importance of classifying adverse drug reactions based on seriousness and expectedness. Three cases were classified as serious, while two were non-serious. Similarly, three reactions were expected and two were unexpected.
